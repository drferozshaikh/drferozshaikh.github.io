All statutory maritime medical decisions are guided by international conventions and national regulations. However operational interpretation involves professional judgement based on vessel type, rank, voyage duration, medication stability and emergency management feasibility at sea.

1. Core International Framework

ILO / IMO Guidelines for the Medical Examination of Seafarers (2013 Edition)
Primary international reference document outlining medical standards, fitness principles and operational risk assessment framework used globally.

Download Official ILO/IMO Guidelines (PDF)
Maritime Labour Convention (MLC 2006)
Establishes the requirement for valid medical certification and outlines minimum health standards for seafarers globally.

View MLC 2006 Information
STCW Convention (Standards of Training Certification and Watchkeeping)
Includes provisions relating to medical fitness for watchkeeping and operational safety.

View STCW Overview

2. Indian Regulatory Framework

Directorate General of Shipping (India) – Medical Examination Rules
Applicable to Indian CDC holders undergoing PEME or REME in DG Shipping approved clinics.

Visit DG Shipping Official Website

3. Flag State & Operational Variability

P&I Club Considerations – American Club (PEME Guidance)
Protection & Indemnity Clubs monitor repatriation risk and medical claims. Their PEME guidance helps shipowners manage medical risk.

View American Club PEME Guidance

P&I Clubs do not issue fitness certificates but influence employer risk policies.
Many Indian seafarers serve on vessels under various flag administrations such as UK, Panama, Liberia and others.

Operational thresholds may vary depending on:

• Flag administration requirements
• Employer risk tolerance
• Vessel trading area
• Rank and safety-critical duties
• P&I risk exposure considerations

Two approved medical examiners may legally reach different conclusions within regulatory allowance based on structured risk assessment.
If you wish to understand how these guidelines may apply to your individual situation you may use the PEME Risk Self-Assessment Tool for structured awareness.

Important Clarification

Guidelines provide a framework — not automatic outcomes. Diagnosis alone does not determine fitness. Operational risk, stability, treatment compliance and emergency feasibility at sea are integral to final certification decisions.

Professional Note: This page is provided for educational reference only. Final fitness certification always rests with the examining authority in accordance with applicable flag and employer regulations.
