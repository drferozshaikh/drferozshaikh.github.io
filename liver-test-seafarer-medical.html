Can High Liver Enzymes Fail Seafarer Medical?

One of the most common worries before a ship medical examination (PEME) is abnormal liver test results. Candidates often see SGPT, SGOT or GGT elevated and fear permanent rejection. In reality, maritime fitness decisions are based on operational risk — not just the lab number.

Why liver tests matter at sea

Ship duty involves long voyages without specialist doctors onboard. Medical decisions focus on predicting whether a condition may suddenly worsen during voyage. The concern with liver abnormalities is not the report itself but:

  • Risk of acute illness during contract
  • Medication safety at sea
  • Alcohol related complications
  • Metabolic syndrome and cardiac risk
Most candidates with mild abnormal reports are NOT permanently rejected. They are usually deferred until stability is confirmed.

Common scenarios and likely outcomes

1. Mild SGPT / SGOT elevation

Usually temporary unfit. Repeat test after rest, diet control or medication adjustment. Very common in young candidates.

2. Fatty liver on ultrasound

Rarely disqualifies alone. Decision depends on weight, sugar levels and long-term risk.

3. High GGT (possible alcohol effect)

Often leads to repeat testing period. Persistent elevation may delay joining but not always career ending.

4. Persistently high values

Requires evaluation for chronic liver disease. Only advanced disease affects long-term eligibility.

Temporary unfit vs permanent unfit

Most liver cases fall into temporary unfit category — meaning improvement allows re-assessment. Permanent unfit is uncommon and usually linked to advanced disease or high complication risk.

Key point: PEME does not judge past illness — it predicts future risk during voyage.

Before spending money on medical

Many candidates travel long distance for examination without knowing whether abnormal results need correction first. Understanding likely interpretation helps avoid repeated medical fees and delays.
