Common Questions

Declared UNFIT — what does it actually mean?

It does not always end a career. Often it reflects risk prediction for specific vessel conditions rather than permanent inability to work.

Different clinics giving different results?

Guidelines allow interpretation. Vessel type, flag rules and job role influence decision thresholds.

Can I join after surgery?

Return depends on recovery stability, recurrence risk and emergency management feasibility at sea.

Common Seafarer Medical Concerns Explained

High Blood Pressure: Mild hypertension often leads to temporary deferral rather than rejection. Stability and treatment reliability are key for sea duty.
Borderline Sugar / HbA1c: Controlled diabetes may be acceptable if hypoglycaemia risk and long voyage safety are manageable.
Eyesight / Colour Vision / LASIK: Standards vary by role. Deck duties require stricter colour perception than engine roles.
High BMI / Fatty Liver: Obesity alone rarely disqualifies, but associated metabolic risk affects clearance.
ECG / TMT Findings: Usually requires evaluation of sudden incapacity risk rather than automatic career rejection.
Liver Enzymes Elevated: Often temporary unfit until cause clarified and values stabilise.
Temporary vs Permanent Unfit: Temporary allows re‑assessment after improvement; permanent depends on long‑term safety risk.
Previous Surgery Disclosure: Non‑disclosure risks complications later; recurrence stability matters more than history.
Dental / Minor Issues: Usually deferred until infection risk is minimised before joining.
Drug Screening Detection: Outcome depends on confirmation testing and company policy.
