Common Real-Life Medical Situations Indian Seafarers Face

Case 1 — Fatty Liver & High SGPT

A 26-year-old engine cadet from Mumbai was declared temporarily unfit after SGPT was found elevated during PEME. He had no symptoms but frequent oily food, alcohol intake and obesity contributed to fatty liver changes. After weight reduction, repeat liver tests and proper preparation, he later cleared his repeat medical.

Case 2 — High Blood Pressure During Medical

An experienced chief cook failed his first reading because of anxiety, poor sleep and excessive tea before PEME. Repeat controlled readings and proper BP management later improved his medical outcome.

Case 3 — Colour Vision Concern

A deck cadet aspirant discovered colour vision deficiency during medical screening. Understanding department-specific rules helped him explore realistic alternatives instead of repeatedly spending money on unsuitable applications.

Case 4 — Borderline Sugar / HbA1c

A cruise ship candidate was worried after borderline sugar levels appeared during pre-employment testing. Lifestyle correction, follow-up reports and medical documentation helped clarify his operational fitness status.

Case 5 — Post-Surgery Return to Sea

A fitter returning after appendix surgery was unsure how long recovery should be before repeat PEME. Understanding recovery timelines and complication risks helped him prepare properly before re-application.

Before You Book Your Seafarer Medical (PEME)

  • Many candidates travel long distances and spend significant money before knowing their realistic fitness outcome.
  • This service provides a pre‑examination eligibility interpretation based on your medical history and reports.
  • You receive a probability‑based opinion to help decide whether to proceed, defer, or clarify specific parameters before attending a clinic.
  • Each submission is reviewed as a single individual case. Multiple persons require separate submissions.

How Medical Fitness Decisions Are Interpreted

  • Fitness for sea duty is not based on diagnosis alone. Operational role, watchkeeping duties, medication stability, voyage duration and repatriation risk are assessed together.
  • Two doctors may legally reach different decisions depending on operational interpretation — this service explains the reasoning behind such outcomes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you guarantee medical fitness?

No. Final fitness decisions remain with the examining medical authority.

Can you influence PEME results?

No. This service is independent and educational in nature.

Can different PEME doctors have different interpretations?

Yes. Operational risk assessment may vary depending on case details and applicable guidelines.

Should medical conditions be hidden during PEME?

No. Honest disclosure is always advised for long-term safety and compliance.

When You Should NOT Use This Service

  • Medication prescription or treatment changes — requires direct clinical consultation.
  • Legal disputes or compensation claims — this is an advisory opinion service only.
