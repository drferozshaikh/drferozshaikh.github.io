Temporary Unfit in Seafarer Medical – What It Actually Means

Many candidates panic after seeing the words “Temporary Unfit” on their seafarer medical report. It often feels like rejection — but in maritime medical practice it usually means the opposite.

Temporary Unfit means: You are NOT permanently rejected. You need correction, observation or stability before joining.

Why doctors give Temporary Unfit

Ship duty requires predictable health stability for long voyages. If a condition might change suddenly, the safest decision is to wait and re-assess rather than approve immediately.

Common reasons:
  • Recent illness or surgery
  • Borderline lab results
  • High blood pressure during examination
  • Abnormal ECG requiring evaluation
  • Elevated sugar or liver tests

Temporary Unfit vs Permanent Unfit

Temporary unfit = condition expected to stabilize Permanent unfit = long-term operational risk

Most candidates declared temporary unfit eventually become fit after proper correction.

Re-medical timing

The waiting period depends on the condition. It is not punishment — it allows demonstration of stability and safety for voyage duration.

Common mistake candidates make

Many immediately repeat medical without correcting the underlying issue. This often leads to repeated unfit reports and unnecessary expense. Understanding interpretation helps plan reassessment correctly.
